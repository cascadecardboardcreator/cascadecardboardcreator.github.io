Support

Found a bug, spotted a typo or a find in the wrong bin, or have an idea? We'd love to hear from you.

Get in touch

Email bugjugapp@gmail.com. It helps to tell us your device, which job or screen you were on, and what you expected to happen. If you use GitHub, you can also open an issue.

Common questions

What ages is it for?

Makers of about 6 to 9. Every line is spoken, so kids who are just starting to read can play on their own. There is nothing to read against a clock and nothing to lose.

We can't find the last thing a job needs.

Take the wagon for another walk. Whatever the job still needs is sure to turn up at a stop that has it, marked with a little sparkle. Tidy a stop completely (sort everything on the ground) and it has new things next time. Neighbors also hand over two of something when you tap them.

The wagon is full.

It holds twelve things. Tap School on the map to roll back and unload onto the shelf, then head out again. Things you can't carry can still go in the right bin.

Why did it say the pizza box is compost?

The bins follow Seattle Public Utilities' rules. Recycling has to be empty, clean and dry; food and food-soiled paper (greasy pizza boxes, napkins) go in the compost; plastic bags, chip bags and foam are garbage; and a loose bottle cap is too small to recycle. Your town may sort differently, so check your own city's guide.

A build failed its test. Did we do something wrong?

No. Every job has a choice with a few options, and the ones that don't work are there to be tried. Flap says what happened, you tap Try another way, and the build keeps the parts you already put on.

Where is Free Make?

It opens when all ten jobs are done: the Build! button in the Maker Room becomes Free Make. Pieces come off the Maker Room shelf and go back when you take them off, so a Wagon Walk restocks it. Parts from the school's maker kit (battery, motor, speaker, microphone, tiny computer, light) and stickers are always free.

How do we play with something we built?

Tap Showcase in the Maker Room, then tap a build. Each one has its own game, and a Paint it button to repaint it. A Free Make invention that passes its test on the lake, the hill or in the sky gets a button to race, drive or fly it there.

How do I turn off the voice or the music?

The two buttons at the top of the title screen switch the spoken lines and the music (with the sound effects) on and off. The home button in the Maker Room goes back to the title screen.

The voice sounds robotic, or doesn't speak.

The lines are read by your device's own speech voice. Check that the device isn't muted and the volume is up. On iPhone and iPad, a better voice can be downloaded in Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices.

How do I move progress to a new phone or tablet?

Progress is saved only on the device it was played on, and there is no way to move it yet. A full device backup and restore (iCloud or Google) may bring it along.

Does it need the internet?

No. Cascade Cardboard Creator works fully offline, even in airplane mode.

Terms

Cascade Cardboard Creator is a game about building and recycling. The tests in it are simple rules of thumb made for play, not engineering advice, and its sorting rules are a guide to Seattle's, not an official source. Check your own city's rules before you sort.

In the game, a grown-up goes on every walk, and the kids never touch broken glass or reach into bins. Please do the same in real life: collect and build with a grown-up, use clean materials from your own home, and ask before taking anything that isn't yours.

The neighborhood in the game is inspired by real public places in Seattle's Cascade neighborhood. The characters, the school and every business are made up, and any resemblance to real people or businesses is a coincidence.
