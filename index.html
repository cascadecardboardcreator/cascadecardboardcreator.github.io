The supply closet is empty.
The neighborhood is full of boxes.

Showcase day is coming to Cascade Playground, and the Maker Room has nothing to build with. So Tully grabs her wagon. With her friend Nico and Nana Jo, she collects clean cardboard, tubes and bottle caps from around the neighborhood, sorts everything else into the right bin, and builds a robot, a streetcar, a boat that floats and a plane that glides. Then she tests each one, fixes it and tries again.

Collect

Take the wagon for a walk.

Eight stops open up around the neighborhood, from Cascade Playground and the P-Patch garden to the streetcar stop and Lake Union Park. Each one has things on the ground, and a neighbor with something to share.

  • Clean boxes, tubes, bottles, cans, caps, egg cartons and newspaper go in the wagon for building.
  • Whatever the current job still needs is sure to turn up, so nobody hunts long for the last box.
  • Neighbors hand over gifts: egg cartons from the gardener, boxes from the loading dock, tent-pole tubes from the outdoor shop.
  • The wagon holds twelve things. Then it rolls back to school.

Sort

Where does it go?

Not everything on the ground is for building. Each find gets a choice: the wagon, the blue bin, the green bin, the gray bin, or leave it alone and tell Nana Jo.

  • The rules are Seattle's real ones: recycling has to be empty, clean and dry; food and greasy pizza boxes are compost; chip bags and foam are garbage.
  • A half-full juice bottle gets emptied first. A sticky yogurt cup gets rinsed.
  • A wrong bin is never a problem. It greys out, Tully gives a hint, and you pick again.

Build

Ten jobs, piece by piece.

Back in the Maker Room, Ms. Hazel's job board says what to build next. Each build goes together a step at a time: tap a piece or drag it onto its spot.

  • The first job is Flap the Box Bot, who then helps with every build after him.
  • A maker kit from the school's drawer shows how things really work: a tiny computer for Flap's brain, a battery for power, a speaker so he can talk, motors to move his arms.
  • Every build has one engineer's choice. Which feet help a robot stand? Which wheels roll? What floats?

Test and improve

Square wheels? Let's find out.

No choice is wrong. Whatever you pick gets built and tested: a wobble, a drizzle, a ramp, a tub of water, a glide across the room or a load of cans.

  • If it doesn't pass, Flap says what happened ("Clunk, clunk! Corners get stuck.") and you try another way.
  • "That is how makers learn" is the only thing anyone says about a failed test.
  • When it works, paint it any way you like. Flap wears his paint everywhere he goes.

Play

Then play with what you made.

Every finished build goes on the Showcase shelf with a game of its own, and the thing you built, painted your way, is the game piece.

  • Race your sailboat across Lake Union. Fly your seaplane over the city.
  • Drive the streetcar, catch marbles, ring the bell tower, climb the rock.
  • Ten games, none with a timer and none you can lose.

Free Make

After the ten jobs, invent your own.

When the job board is empty, the Maker Room is yours. Any piece goes anywhere on the mat: turn it, grow it, shrink it, paint it.

  • Six places to try an invention out: the wobble table, a rainy garden, the playground hill, Lake Union, the windy sky and the showcase stage.
  • The tests follow simple, honest rules. Wide bottoms stand. Round wheels roll. Bottles float. Wings over the middle glide.
  • Neighbors pin wishes on the Wish Board. A wish come true wins a sticker to build with.
  • Motors, lights and googly eyes are always in the drawer.

Ten jobs on the board

Each one teaches one idea about how things stand, roll, float, fly or hold a load.

  1. Flap the Box BotEvery Maker Room needs a helper. Which feet will help him stand?
  2. P-Patch Bug HotelA dry, cozy home for ladybugs. Which roof keeps out the Seattle rain?
  3. Lake Union StreetcarIt has to really roll. Square wheels, triangle wheels or round ones?
  4. Lake Union SailboatA newspaper hull, a tall box or two bottles. What floats?
  5. SeaplaneTiny wings, wings at the back or big wings in the middle. What glides?
  6. Mercer Street BridgeIt has to carry a load of cans. Triangles turn out to be strong.
  7. Climbing RockA tall rock that won't tip over needs the right base.
  8. Onion Dome TowerHow do you make flat paper round?
  9. Marble CascadeA waterfall of ramps for a marble. Which way should they tilt?
  10. The Cardboard CascadesShowcase day: build the mountains, as big as you dare.

For grown-ups

Cascade Cardboard Creator was made by a parent in Seattle for their own young maker, as a sister game to Maple Leaf Math and Licton Springs Language. Here's what you can expect.

🔧 The engineering loop

Plan, build, test, improve. Every job has a choice that gets tested, and the options that don't work are part of the fun, not a mistake.

♻️ Sorting that's true

The bins follow Seattle Public Utilities' rules, including the tricky ones: a greasy pizza box is compost, a loose bottle cap is too small to recycle. Your town may sort differently, which is a good thing to talk about.

🧤 Safety, modeled right

A grown-up is on every walk. The kids never touch broken glass, never reach into bins, stay on the sidewalk, and ask before taking anything near a neighbor's things.

🗣️ Every line is spoken

Everything a character says is read aloud by your device's own voice, so kids who are just learning to read can play on their own. You can turn the voice or the music off.

⏳ No pressure

No timers, no streaks, no scores to beat, no loot boxes. A race cheers whatever place you come in.

🔒 Private by design

No ads, accounts, analytics or tracking, and the app never goes online. Progress is saved only on the device. Privacy policy

🏙️ A real neighborhood

Cascade Playground, the P-Patch and Lake Union Park are real public places. Every business, school and person in the game is made up.

📦 Then try it for real

Everything in the game is built from things a family already has: boxes, tubes, bottle caps and tape. The best outcome is a kid asking for the recycling bin.
